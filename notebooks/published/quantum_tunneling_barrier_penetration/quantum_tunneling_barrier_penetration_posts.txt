Title: I built a quantum tunneling simulator in Python - here's what I learned about wave functions passing through barriers

Body:

**What is quantum tunneling?**

In classical physics, if you don't have enough energy to climb over a hill, you can't get to the other side. Period. But in quantum mechanics, particles can "tunnel" through barriers even when their energy E is less than the barrier height V0.

**The math (simplified)**

The probability of tunneling through depends on:
- Barrier height (V0)
- Barrier width (a)
- Particle energy (E)
- Particle mass (m)

The key result is the transmission coefficient T, which for thick barriers approximates to:

T ~ e^(-2ka)

where k = sqrt(2m(V0-E))/h-bar is the decay constant.

This exponential dependence is crucial - doubling barrier width can reduce transmission by many orders of magnitude.

**What I simulated**

- Electron tunneling through a 5 eV barrier, 1 nm wide
- At E = 2 eV (below barrier), T ~ 10^-4
- Visualized wave function: oscillating outside, exponentially decaying inside
- Showed resonance peaks when E > V0

**Real-world applications**

This isn't just academic - quantum tunneling enables:
- Scanning tunneling microscopy (imaging atoms!)
- Tunnel diodes in electronics
- Alpha decay in nuclear physics
- Josephson junctions in superconducting circuits
