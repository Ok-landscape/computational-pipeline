**Title:** I built a spectral solver with Chebyshev polynomials - here's why they achieve exponential convergence

**Body:**

Ever wondered why weather prediction models and turbulence simulations use spectral methods instead of finite differences? I just implemented one from scratch and finally understand the magic.

**The Problem with Finite Differences**

Finite difference methods approximate derivatives locally: f'(x) ≈ (f(x+h) - f(x-h))/(2h). The error is O(h²) or O(h⁴) - you need LOTS of points for high accuracy.

**Enter Chebyshev Polynomials**

Chebyshev polynomials T_n(x) are defined by a beautiful identity: T_n(x) = cos(n*arccos(x)). They satisfy a simple recurrence:

- T₀(x) = 1
- T₁(x) = x
- Tn₊₁(x) = 2x*T_n(x) - Tn₋₁(x)

**Why They're Special**

1. They're orthogonal with respect to weight w(x) = 1/sqrt(1-x²)
2. The Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto points xⱼ = cos(pij/N) cluster near boundaries, avoiding the Runge phenomenon
3. You can build a differentiation MATRIX - derivatives become matrix-vector multiplication!

**The Results**

I solved the BVP: -u'' + u = (2+4x)e^x with u(-1) = u(1) = 0.

| N | Max Error |
|---|-----------|
| 8 | 2.5 x 10⁻5 |
| 16 | 1.8 x 10⁻10 |
| 32 | 2.2 x 10⁻14 |

That's **exponential convergence** - errors decay like e⁽-cN) instead of h^p. For smooth problems, spectral methods are unbeatable.

Key Technical Insights:

1. Chebyshev polynomials T_n(x) = cos(n*arccos(x)) form an optimal interpolation basis on bounded domains

2. The Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto points xⱼ = cos(pi*j/N) naturally cluster near boundaries, eliminating the Runge phenomenon

- N=8: error ~ 10⁻5
