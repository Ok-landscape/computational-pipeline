Title: Exploring Hypergeometric Functions: The "Universal" Special Functions

Body:

I created a computational notebook exploring hypergeometric functions - one of the most important (and underappreciated) classes of special functions in mathematics.

**What are hypergeometric functions?**

The Gaussian hypergeometric function ₂F₁(a,b;c;z) is defined by:

₂F₁(a,b;c;z) = ∑ [(a)ₙ(b)ₙ / (c)ₙ] × zⁿ/n!

where (q)ₙ is the Pochhammer symbol (rising factorial).

**Why should you care?**

Here's the cool part - tons of "different" functions are actually just hypergeometric functions with special parameters:

| Function | Hypergeometric Form |
|----------|-------------------|
| (1-z)⁻ᵃ | ₂F₁(a,b;b;z) |
| ln(1+z) | z × ₂F₁(1,1;2;-z) |
| arcsin(z) | z × ₂F₁(1/2,1/2;3/2;z²) |
| Legendre Pₙ(z) | ₂F₁(-n,n+1;1;(1-z)/2) |

**What I implemented:**

1. Series computation from scratch using the Pochhammer symbol
2. Verification against SciPy's hyp2f1 (errors < 10⁻¹⁰)
3. Visualization of different parameter choices
4. Verified Euler's and Pfaff's transformation formulas
5. Applied to hydrogen atom radial wavefunctions

**Key insight:** The hypergeometric differential equation z(1-z)w'' + [c-(a+b+1)z]w' - abw = 0 has exactly three regular singular points, making ₂F₁ the canonical solution for this class of ODEs.
