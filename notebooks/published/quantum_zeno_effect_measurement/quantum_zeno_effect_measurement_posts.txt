**Title:** I simulated the Quantum Zeno Effect in Python - watching a quantum system can freeze it in place

**Body:**

The Quantum Zeno Effect is one of the wildest things in quantum mechanics: if you measure a quantum system frequently enough, you can stop it from evolving. It's named after Zeno of Elea and his paradoxes about motion.

**ELI5 version:** Imagine a pot of water trying to boil. Every time you check on it ("a watched pot never boils"), you reset it to cold. Check often enough, and it never gets hot. That's essentially what happens at the quantum level, except it's real physics, not just a saying.

**The math (simplified):**
- A quantum system oscillates between states with probability P(t) = cos²(Ωt/2)
- With n measurements over time T, survival probability becomes P_n = [cos²(ΩT/2n)]^n
- As n → ∞, P_n → 1 (the system is "frozen")

**What I learned:**
- The effect relies on quadratic short-time decay (not exponential)
- Decay suppression scales as 1/n with measurement count
- Monte Carlo simulation matches analytical predictions beautifully
- There's also an Anti-Zeno Effect where measurements can accelerate decay!

The notebook includes derivations from the Schrödinger equation, Monte Carlo simulations, and visualizations showing how measurement frequency affects quantum evolution.
