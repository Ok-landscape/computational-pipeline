Deep dive into Stochastic Differential Equations:

The general Ito SDE: dXt = mu(Xt,t)dt + sigma(Xt,t)dWt

Where mu is drift (deterministic trend) and sigma is diffusion (noise amplitude).

Implemented both Euler-Maruyama and Milstein schemes in Python. Key findings:
- Euler-Maruyama: strong order 1/2, error ~ O(sqrt(dt))
- Milstein: strong order 1, error ~ O(dt)

The Milstein correction term: (1/2)*sigma*sigma'*[(dW)^2 - dt]

**Body:**

Hey everyone!

I've been learning about Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and wanted to share my implementation and what I learned.

**What are SDEs?**

Think of regular differential equations (ODEs) as describing how things change over time deterministically. SDEs add randomness to the mix using something called a Wiener process (basically mathematical Brownian motion).

The general form is:

dX = mu(X,t)dt + sigma(X,t)dW

Where:
- mu is the "drift" (predictable trend)
- sigma is the "diffusion" (how much randomness)
- dW is a random increment from the Wiener process

**What I implemented:**

1. **Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM)** - Used in the Black-Scholes model for stock prices. The cool thing: the solution is lognormally distributed!

2. **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process** - Models mean-reverting behavior. Great for interest rates or anything that tends to return to an average value.

3. **Numerical Methods:**
   - Euler-Maruyama (simple but converges slowly at O(sqrt(dt)))
   - Milstein (adds a correction term, converges at O(dt))

**Key takeaway:**

Milstein's method adds this correction term: (1/2)*sigma*sigma'*[(dW)^2 - dt]

This accounts for Ito's lemma (the stochastic version of the chain rule) and dramatically improves accuracy.
