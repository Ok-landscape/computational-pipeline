TITLE: I built a Python notebook exploring the partition function — the "master key" of statistical mechanics

BODY:

**ELI5:** Imagine you have a box full of particles bouncing around. The partition function is like a magic calculator that, given just the energies of all possible arrangements, tells you *everything* about the system — average energy, pressure, heat capacity, you name it.

**What is the partition function?**

Z = ∑ᵢ e^(-βEᵢ)

That's it. Sum over all possible states, weight each by e^(-E/k_B T). From this one number:
- Free energy: F = -k_B T × ln(Z)
- Average energy: ⟨E⟩ = -∂(ln Z)/∂β
- Heat capacity: C_V = β² × variance of energy

**What I implemented:**

1. **Two-level system** — The simplest quantum system. Shows the "Schottky anomaly," a heat capacity peak when thermal energy matches the energy gap.

2. **Quantum harmonic oscillator** — Energy levels E_n = ℏω(n + 1/2). At low T, only the ground state matters; at high T, classical equipartition kicks in.

3. **Ideal gas** — Derived PV = Nk_B T directly from Z! Also explored the thermal de Broglie wavelength — when it approaches atomic spacing, quantum effects emerge.

4. **1D Ising model** — Spins on a chain with nearest-neighbor interactions. Used the transfer matrix method for exact solution. Fun fact: no phase transition in 1D at finite temperature!

**What I learned:**

The partition function isn't just a mathematical trick — it's a *generating function*. Differentiate ln(Z) with respect to β and you get ⟨E⟩. Differentiate again for variance. It's elegant.
