Instead of solving differential equations, you can use algebraic operators:
- a (annihilation): lowers energy state by one quantum
- a+ (creation): raises energy state by one quantum

The energy comes out as E_n = hbar*omega*(n + 1/2) - that 1/2 is the famous zero-point energy!

Title: Built a Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Simulation Using Ladder Operators in Python

Body:

I created a Jupyter notebook exploring one of the most elegant approaches to quantum mechanics: solving the harmonic oscillator with ladder operators instead of differential equations.

**What are ladder operators?**

Instead of solving Schrodinger's equation directly, we define two operators:
- Annihilation operator (a): lowers the quantum number by 1
- Creation operator (a+): raises the quantum number by 1

These satisfy [a, a+] = 1, and the Hamiltonian becomes simply H = hbar*omega*(a+*a + 1/2).

**Key findings from the simulation:**

1. Energy levels E_n = hbar*omega*(n + 1/2) emerge naturally
2. Ground state (n=0) saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty bound: Dx*Dp = hbar/2
3. Position and momentum operators can be expressed as combinations of a and a+
4. Coherent states |alpha> (eigenstates of a) behave most classically

**What I learned:**

The ladder operator approach shows why quantum mechanics has discrete energy levels - it's built into the algebraic structure. The "1/2" in the energy formula (zero-point energy) means even the ground state has non-zero energy and motion.

The code uses NumPy for matrix representations of operators in truncated Fock space, SciPy for Hermite polynomials, and Matplotlib for visualization.

Quantum ladders

In quantum mechanics, you can climb energy levels using "ladder operators":

a+ takes you UP one level
a takes you DOWN one level

The energy at each rung:
E_n = hbar*omega*(n + 1/2)

That "+1/2" is zero-point energy - quantum systems are never truly at rest.

This simple idea underlies:
- Molecular vibrations
- Photons in light
- Quantum computing
