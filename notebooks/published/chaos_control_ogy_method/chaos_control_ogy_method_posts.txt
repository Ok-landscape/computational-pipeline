**Title:** Controlling Chaos: Implemented the OGY Method to Stabilize Unstable Orbits

**Body:**

The Ott-Grebogi-Yorke (OGY) method, published in 1990, revolutionized chaos theory by showing that chaotic systems can be controlled with remarkably small perturbations.

**The Counterintuitive Insight**

Chaotic systems seem unpredictable and uncontrollable. But the OGY method exploits a beautiful fact: every chaotic attractor contains infinitely many unstable periodic orbits (UPOs) embedded within the chaotic sea.

The same sensitivity that makes chaos unpredictable also makes it controllable—small perturbations can have large effects when applied at the right time.

**The Mathematics**

Consider a discrete map: x_{n+1} = F(x_n, p) where p is a controllable parameter.

Near an unstable fixed point x_F, linearization gives:
x_{n+1} - x_F ≈ J(x_n - x_F) + g(p_n - p_0)

The Jacobian J has:
- Stable eigenvalue λ_s with |λ_s| < 1 (shrinks deviations)
- Unstable eigenvalue λ_u with |λ_u| > 1 (amplifies deviations)

**The Control Strategy**

Choose parameter perturbation δp to place the next iterate on the stable manifold. This requires:

1. Compute eigenvectors e_s (stable) and e_u (unstable)
2. Find contravariant eigenvector f_u (left eigenvector for unstable direction)
3. Calculate control gain: K = (λ_u/denominator) × f_u
4. Apply feedback: δp = -K·(x - x_F) when close to target

**Implementation on Hénon Map**

The Hénon map is a classic 2D chaotic system:
- x_{n+1} = 1 - ax²_n + y_n
- y_{n+1} = bx_n

With a=1.4, b=0.3, it exhibits a strange attractor.

Fixed point: (0.631, 0.189)
Eigenvalues: λ_s = 0.156 (stable), λ_u = -1.924 (unstable)
Control gain: K = [2.911, -1.513]

**Results**

- Control activated at iteration 500
- Trajectory wandered chaotically until entering control radius
- Stabilization achieved in just 31 iterations!
- Parameter perturbations: mean |δa| = 0.000186, max = 0.05
- Final distance from fixed point: ~10⁻⁶

**Why This Matters**

The OGY method demonstrated that:
1. Chaos is not synonymous with uncontrollability
2. Small interventions can achieve large goals
3. The dense set of UPOs in chaotic systems provides abundant control targets

**Real-World Applications**

- **Laser dynamics**: Stabilizing irregular pulsations
- **Cardiac control**: Preventing arrhythmias
- **Chemical reactions**: Controlling oscillating reactions
- **Mechanical systems**: Suppressing chaotic vibrations

**Limitations**

- Need to wait for trajectory to naturally approach target (waiting time)
- Requires accurate state measurement
- Sensitive to noise (led to development of time-delay feedback)
- Single-parameter control limits applicability

The full implementation with visualizations shows phase space trajectories, time series, and parameter perturbations. It's amazing to see chaos tamed with such minimal intervention!

• Contravariant eigenvector computation for feedback gain
