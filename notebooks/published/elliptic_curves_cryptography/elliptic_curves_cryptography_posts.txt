**TL;DR:** Built a complete ECC implementation including point addition, scalar multiplication, and Diffie-Hellman key exchange. The math is beautiful and the security comes from a simple geometric operation being computationally irreversible.

**What are Elliptic Curves?**

An elliptic curve is defined by: y² = x³ + ax + b

Nothing to do with ellipses! The name comes from elliptic integrals. The cool part: points on this curve form a mathematical group.

**The Group Operation**

To "add" two points P and Q:
1. Draw a line through them
2. Find where it intersects the curve (always a third point)
3. Reflect that point over the x-axis

That's it. This geometric operation, when done in a finite field (mod p), creates a one-way function.

**Why It's Secure**

Given points P and Q where Q = kP (meaning you added P to itself k times), finding k is the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP).

Best known attack: O(√n) - meaning 256-bit keys give 128-bit security.

**Key Size Comparison:**
- 256-bit ECC = 3072-bit RSA
- 384-bit ECC = 7680-bit RSA

Same security, much smaller keys = faster operations and less bandwidth.
