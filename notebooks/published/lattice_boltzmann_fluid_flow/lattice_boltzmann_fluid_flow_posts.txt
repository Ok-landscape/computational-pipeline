4. MASTODON (< 500 characters)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

New simulation: Lattice Boltzmann Method for 2D viscous flow

D2Q9 lattice with BGK collision operator:
f_i^out = f_i - omega(f_i - f_i^eq)

where omega = 1/tau and viscosity nu = c_s^2(tau - 1/2)

At Re=100, the cylinder wake shows classic von Karman instability. Strouhal number St approx 0.18 matches experimental data.

Boundary conditions:
- Zou-He at inlet
- Bounce-back on obstacle
- Zero-gradient outlet

Code: pure NumPy, 400x100 lattice, 15k steps

**Title:** I built a fluid dynamics simulator using the Lattice Boltzmann Method in pure Python/NumPy - here's how it works

**Body:**

Hey everyone! I've been learning about computational fluid dynamics and wanted to share a project that finally made the concepts click for me.

**What is the Lattice Boltzmann Method?**

Instead of directly solving the Navier-Stokes equations (which describe fluid motion), LBM works at a "mesoscopic" scale. Imagine tracking probabilities of finding particles moving in certain directions on a grid. Two simple steps repeat:

1. **Collision** - particles at each point redistribute according to local equilibrium
2. **Streaming** - particles move to neighboring grid points

That's it! From these simple rules, complex fluid behavior emerges.

**The simulation**

I simulated flow around a cylinder (think: wind past a bridge pillar). At Reynolds number 100, something beautiful happens - the wake becomes unstable and starts shedding vortices alternately from top and bottom. This is called the "von Karman vortex street."

**Key equations (in plain English)**

- Equilibrium distribution: basically a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution discretized onto the lattice
- Viscosity: controlled by the "relaxation time" tau - how quickly particles return to equilibrium
- Strouhal number: relates shedding frequency to flow speed and obstacle size (got St around 0.18, matching experiments!)

**What I learned**

- Bounce-back boundaries naturally create no-slip walls
- The D2Q9 lattice (9 velocities in 2D) is surprisingly powerful
- LBM is embarrassingly parallel - great for GPU acceleration

The whole thing runs in pure NumPy. No fancy CFD libraries needed!
