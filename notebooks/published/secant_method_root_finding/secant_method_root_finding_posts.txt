The Secant Method: A derivative-free root-finding algorithm with superlinear convergence.

Instead of computing f'(x), it approximates derivatives using finite differences:
xn₊₁ = xₙ - f(xₙ) × (xₙ - xₙ₋₁)/(f(xₙ) - f(xₙ₋₁))

Convergence order: φ ≈ 1.618 (the golden ratio!)

**Title:** I implemented the Secant Method in Python - a clever root-finding algorithm that doesn't need derivatives

**Body:**

Hey r/learnpython!

I just finished implementing the Secant Method for finding roots of equations, and wanted to share what I learned.

**What is it?**

The Secant Method finds where f(x) = 0 without needing the derivative f'(x). Instead of using calculus, it approximates the derivative using two points:

f'(xₙ) ≈ (f(xₙ) - f(xₙ₋₁))/(xₙ - xₙ₋₁)

**Why is it cool?**

1. **No derivatives needed** - Great when f'(x) is hard to compute
2. **Superlinear convergence** - The convergence order is the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618
3. **Simple to implement** - Just a few lines of Python

**Example results:**

Finding √2 by solving x² - 2 = 0:
- Converged in 6 iterations
- Final accuracy: 10⁻¹⁵ (machine precision!)

**The trade-off:**

It's slower than Newton's method (order 2) but faster than bisection (order 1). Perfect middle ground when you can't compute derivatives.
