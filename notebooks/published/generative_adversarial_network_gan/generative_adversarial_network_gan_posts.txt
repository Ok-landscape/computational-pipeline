Implemented a GAN from scratch in NumPy to understand the fundamentals.

Key insight: The minimax objective min_G max_D V(D,G) reformulates as minimizing Jensen-Shannon divergence:
C(G) = -log(4) + 2·D_JS(p_data ‖ p_g)

At Nash equilibrium: p_g = p_data and D*(x) = 1/2 everywhere.

Used non-saturating loss (max log D(G(z))) for better gradients. Successfully learned N(4.0, 1.25) with accurate mean and variance recovery.

### Reddit (r/learnpython or r/MachineLearning)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
**Title:** Built a GAN from scratch in NumPy - Here's what I learned about the math behind generative AI

**Body:**
I implemented a Generative Adversarial Network using only NumPy (no PyTorch/TensorFlow) to really understand how they work. Here's the key takeaway:

**The Core Idea (ELI5):**
Imagine a counterfeiter (Generator) trying to make fake money, and a detective (Discriminator) trying to catch them. They keep getting better at their jobs until the counterfeiter makes perfect fakes.

**The Math:**
- Generator G(z) maps random noise z to data
- Discriminator D(x) outputs probability that x is real
- They play a minimax game: min_G max_D V(D,G)
- At equilibrium, D(x) = 1/2 (can't tell real from fake)

**What I Learned:**
1. Non-saturating loss is crucial - using max log(D(G(z))) instead of min log(1-D(G(z))) prevents vanishing gradients
2. The optimal discriminator gives D*(x) = p_data(x) / (p_data(x) + p_g(x))
3. GAN training minimizes Jensen-Shannon divergence between real and generated distributions
4. Xavier initialization matters a lot for stable training

My simple GAN learned to generate samples from N(4.0, 1.25) and matched both mean and variance accurately.

### LinkedIn (< 1000 chars)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deepening my understanding of generative AI by implementing a GAN from first principles.

Generative Adversarial Networks, introduced by Goodfellow et al. (2014), underpin modern AI image generation. To truly understand them, I built one using only NumPy - no deep learning frameworks.

Key technical insights:

The Architecture: Two networks in a minimax game
- Generator G(z; θ_g): Maps latent noise to data space
- Discriminator D(x; θ_d): Classifies real vs. generated

The Objective: min_G max_D E[log D(x)] + E[log(1 - D(G(z)))]

At Nash equilibrium, the generator perfectly matches the data distribution (p_g = p_data), and the discriminator outputs 0.5 for all inputs.

Implementation details:
- Leaky ReLU activations for gradient flow
- Xavier initialization for stable training
- Non-saturating generator loss for stronger gradients

Skills demonstrated: Neural network implementation, backpropagation, optimization theory, statistical validation.
