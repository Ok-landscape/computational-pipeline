### Mastodon (500 chars)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
New notebook: Hidden Markov Models with Viterbi decoding

An HMM is defined by λ = (S, O, A, B, π) where:
- A = transition matrix: aᵢⱼ = P(state_j | state_i)
- B = emission matrix: bⱼ(k) = P(obs_k | state_j)
- π = initial distribution

Viterbi finds Q* = argmax P(Q|X,λ) via dynamic programming:
δₜ(j) = max[δₜ₋₁(i) · aᵢⱼ] · bⱼ(xₜ)

Complexity: O(N²T) time, O(NT) space

Implementation uses log probabilities to prevent underflow. Applied to weather inference from activities.

**Title:** I implemented Hidden Markov Models with Viterbi decoding from scratch - here's what I learned

**Body:**

Ever wondered how speech recognition, DNA sequence analysis, or predictive text work under the hood? Many use Hidden Markov Models (HMMs).

**ELI5 Version:**
Imagine you have a friend who texts you what they did today: "Walked", "Shopped", or "Cleaned". You can't see the weather where they are, but you know:
- They're more likely to walk when it's sunny
- They're more likely to clean when it's rainy

Given a week of their activities, can you guess what the weather was each day? That's exactly what HMMs solve!

**The Math (simplified):**
An HMM has:
- Hidden states (Sunny/Rainy) that follow a Markov chain
- Observable outputs (Walk/Shop/Clean) that depend on the hidden state
- Transition probabilities: P(tomorrow's weather | today's weather)
- Emission probabilities: P(activity | weather)

**The Viterbi Algorithm:**
Instead of greedily picking the most likely state at each step, Viterbi uses dynamic programming to find the globally optimal sequence. The key recurrence:

δₜ(j) = max[δₜ₋₁(i) · aᵢⱼ] · bⱼ(xₜ)

where δₜ(j) is the probability of the best path ending in state j at time t.

**What I Built:**
- Complete Python implementation with NumPy
- Log-probability handling to prevent numerical underflow
- Weather inference demo achieving 70%+ accuracy
- Visualization of the Viterbi trellis and state comparisons

**Key Takeaways:**
1. Use log probabilities for long sequences (avoid underflow)
2. Viterbi gives the globally optimal path, not locally optimal
3. O(N²T) complexity makes it practical for real applications
