Title: Built a Python simulation of the Stern-Gerlach experiment - quantum spin measurement visualized

Body:

**What I built:**

A NumPy-based simulation of spin-1/2 particles in a Stern-Gerlach apparatus, complete with Bloch sphere visualization and deflection pattern plots.

**The physics:**

The Stern-Gerlach experiment (1922) demonstrated that angular momentum is quantized. When you send silver atoms through an inhomogeneous magnetic field, they don't spread out continuously - they split into exactly 2 beams corresponding to spin-up (+ℏ/2) and spin-down (−ℏ/2).

**Cool features of the simulation:**

1. **Pauli matrices** - Implemented σx, σy, σz as 2x2 complex matrices
2. **Bloch sphere parameterization** - Any spin state can be written as |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|+⟩ + e^(iφ)sin(θ/2)|−⟩
3. **Measurement probabilities** - Uses the Born rule to calculate P(↑) and P(↓)
4. **Sequential experiments** - Demonstrates how measuring along x "erases" z-information

**The mind-bending result:**

If you prepare a particle spin-up along z, then measure along x (selecting spin-up), then measure along z again - you can get spin-DOWN! This happens because [Sz, Sx] = iℏSy ≠ 0 (the operators don't commute).

**What I learned:**

- How to work with complex numpy arrays for quantum state vectors
- Monte Carlo simulation of quantum measurements
- 3D visualization with matplotlib's mplot3d
