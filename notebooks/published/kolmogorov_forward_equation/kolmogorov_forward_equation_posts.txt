**Title:** I built a Python solver for the Fokker-Planck equation - here's what I learned about stochastic systems

**Body:**

**ELI5 Version:**
Imagine you drop a ball at the top of a hill. You know exactly where it'll roll. Now imagine dropping millions of tiny balls while someone randomly shakes the hill. Where will they end up?

The Fokker-Planck equation answers this! It tells you how the "cloud" of possible positions evolves over time.

**The Math (in Unicode):**
∂p/∂t = -∂(μp)/∂x + ½∂²(Dp)/∂x²

Where:
- p(x,t) = probability density at position x, time t
- μ(x) = drift (deterministic push)
- D(x) = diffusion (random spreading)

**What I Built:**
A Python class using Crank-Nicolson finite differences (unconditionally stable!) that solves this PDE for arbitrary drift and diffusion functions.

**Three Examples:**

1. **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process** - Models mean-reverting behavior (interest rates, stock prices returning to average). Converges to a Gaussian.

2. **Double-Well Potential** - Think of a ball that can rest in one of two valleys. With noise, it can hop between them! The equilibrium follows the Boltzmann distribution: p ∝ exp(-2V/D).

3. **Geometric Brownian Motion** - The foundation of the Black-Scholes model. State-dependent diffusion gives log-normal distributions.

**Key Insight:**
Individual particles are unpredictable, but their collective probability distribution evolves deterministically. It's order emerging from chaos!

I developed a numerical solver using:
