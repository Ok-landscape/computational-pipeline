Ever wondered how supercomputers solve massive systems of equations so fast?

The answer is multigrid methods! Instead of grinding through millions of iterations, multigrid uses a clever trick:

1. Smooth out the "wiggly" errors on a fine grid
2. Transfer remaining "smooth" errors to a coarser grid
3. Repeat until you hit a tiny grid you can solve directly
4. Interpolate corrections back up

For the Poisson equation, this gives mesh-independent convergence. Mind = blown.

**Title:** I built a multigrid PDE solver from scratch in Python - here's how it achieves O(N) optimal complexity

**Body:**

Ever tried to solve a large system of linear equations with basic iterative methods like Jacobi? You'll wait forever. Here's why multigrid methods are a game-changer.

**The Problem:**
The Poisson equation ∇²u = f shows up everywhere: electrostatics, heat transfer, fluid flow. When you discretize it on a grid, you get a huge sparse linear system.

**Why Simple Methods Fail:**
Iterative methods like Jacobi are great at eliminating "wiggly" high-frequency errors but terrible at smooth, low-frequency ones. The smoother the error, the more iterations you need.

**The Multigrid Insight:**
A smooth error on a fine grid looks wiggly on a coarser grid! So multigrid:

1. Smooths the solution on the fine grid (kills high-freq errors)
2. Computes the residual and restricts it to a coarser grid
3. Recursively solves for the error correction
4. Interpolates the correction back and applies it
5. Smooths again

**Results:**
- 66×66 grid: Multigrid converges in ~15 V-cycles
- Same problem with Jacobi: needs thousands of iterations
- Convergence rate ρ ≈ 0.1 is independent of grid size!
