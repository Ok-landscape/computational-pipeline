**Title:** [OC] Built a Finite Element Method solver for heat transfer in Python - here's how it works

**Body:**

I created a Python notebook implementing the Finite Element Method (FEM) for 1D steady-state heat conduction. FEM is the backbone of engineering simulation software, and building one from scratch really helps understand the fundamentals.

**The Problem:**

We want to solve the heat equation: -k(d²T/dx²) = Q(x)

Where T is temperature, k is thermal conductivity, and Q is a heat source.

**How FEM Works (ELI5):**

1. Chop your rod into small pieces (elements)
2. Assume temperature varies linearly within each piece
3. Use calculus tricks (integration by parts) to convert the differential equation into matrix multiplication
4. Solve the matrix equation KT = f for nodal temperatures

**What I Learned:**

- Linear elements give O(h²) convergence - halving element size reduces error by 4x
- Boundary conditions come in two flavors:
  - Dirichlet: "Temperature at this point is 100°C"
  - Neumann: "Heat flux at this boundary is 50 W/m²"
- Composite materials (like a rod with two different metals) work naturally by varying k per element

**Key Results:**

The FEM solution matches analytical solutions for all test cases. The convergence plot confirms second-order accuracy.
