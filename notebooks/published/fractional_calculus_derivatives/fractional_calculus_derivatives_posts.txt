**Title:** I implemented fractional calculus in Python — here's how "half-derivatives" work

**Body:**

You know how regular calculus gives us the 1st derivative, 2nd derivative, etc.? Fractional calculus asks: what about the 0.5th derivative? Or the 1.7th derivative?

**ELI5 version:** Imagine differentiation as a "slider" — normally it only has integer positions (0, 1, 2...). Fractional calculus lets you stop anywhere on that slider.

**The key formula:**
For f(x) = x^β, the fractional derivative of order α is:

D^α(x^β) = [Γ(β+1) / Γ(β-α+1)] · x^(β-α)

Where Γ is the Gamma function (which generalizes factorials to non-integers).

**Example:** The half-derivative of x:
- D^0.5(x) = Γ(2)/Γ(1.5) · x^0.5 = (2/√π)·√x

**Why does this matter?**
- Viscoelastic materials have "memory" — their stress depends on their entire strain history
- Anomalous diffusion (particles spreading slower/faster than normal)
- PID controllers with fractional orders for better control
- The solutions decay as power laws, not exponentials — capturing real physical behavior better

I used the Grünwald-Letnikov numerical scheme, which approximates the derivative using weighted sums. The weights follow a neat recursive formula, making it efficient to compute.
